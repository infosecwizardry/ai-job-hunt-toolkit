# Summary examples

These examples are written for illustration. Use them to calibrate tone and structure, not as templates to copy.

## Weak

> "Motivated, experienced SOC analyst passionate about protecting organizations and eager to grow my skills."

What's wrong with it:

- It names qualities ("motivated," "passionate") instead of showing them.
- "Experienced SOC analyst" doesn't say what the experience was.
- Nothing connects it to a specific job listing. It could be sent anywhere.
- Nothing tells a hiring manager what this person would be like on the team.

## Strong

> "Help desk technician moving into SOC work. The tickets I liked most were the ones nobody had an answer for yet, and digging until I found one is how I picked up most of what I know about Windows logs and Active Directory. Right now I'm working through Security+ at night, building out a home lab to practice what I learn, and documenting every lab on GitHub. I like being the person the team can hand a weird problem to."

Broken down by part:

| Part | Sentence | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| The role | "Help desk technician moving into SOC work." | States the target plainly, in the first few words. |
| The fit | "The tickets I liked most were the ones nobody had an answer for yet, and digging until I found one is how I picked up most of what I know about Windows logs and Active Directory." | Connects real help desk work to skills a SOC role needs, without overstating it. |
| Who they'd be on the team | "Right now I'm working through Security+ at night, building out a home lab to practice what I learn, and documenting every lab on GitHub. I like being the person the team can hand a weird problem to." | Shows self-driven learning and willingness to chip in through what they actually do. No adjectives. |

## Turning an adjective into evidence

When someone describes themselves with an adjective, ask for the behavior behind it.

| They say | Ask | Becomes something like |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm a fast learner." | "What's the last thing you taught yourself, and how?" | "I taught myself Splunk searches by rebuilding my home lab's logs until I could find my own test attacks." |
| "I'm a team player." | "When did you help someone on your team without being asked?" | "When I figure out a fix, I write it up so the next person doesn't have to." |
| "I'm passionate about security." | "What do you do in security when nobody's making you?" | "I spend a couple of nights a week working through blue team labs and posting the write-ups." |

Only use details the person actually gives you. The right-hand column shows the shape of a good answer, not facts to borrow.
